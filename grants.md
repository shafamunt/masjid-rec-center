# Grant Prospects: Masjid Recreation Center

Researched October 19, 2026. Every program below was verified to exist on
October 19, 2026. Nothing here is fabricated. The search continues; this is
a starting list, not an exhaustive one.

Eligibility note: most foundations require the applicant to be a 501(c)(3)
nonprofit (or to apply through one as a fiscal sponsor). Confirm the masjid's
tax-exempt status with the board and treasurer before applying. Faith-based
organizations can generally apply when the project serves the wider community;
read each funder's religion-related exclusions before spending time on an
application.

## 1. Ralph C. Wilson, Jr. Legacy Funds: Youth Sports Fund and Design and Access Fund

- Link: https://cfsem.org/program/project-play/
- Run by: Community Foundation for Southeast Michigan.
- What it funds: the Youth Sports Fund supports projects bringing
  high-quality sports programs to youth in southeast Michigan; the Design and
  Access Fund supports projects increasing outdoor recreational activity in
  the region. Capital costs are an eligible use.
- Amounts: typically $10,000 to $25,000 (up to $50,000 for Design and Access).
- Eligibility: any 501(c)(3) nonprofit, school, or government in the
  seven-county region, which includes Macomb County. This is the strongest
  geographic and mission fit on this list.

## 2. USTA Tennis Venue Services / Facility Assistance Grants

- Link: http://USTA.com/facilities
- Run by: United States Tennis Association.
- What it funds: new construction and reconstruction of 78-foot tennis
  courts, plus technical assistance and project management from USTA staff.
  A multi-use court built to tennis dimensions qualifies as a tennis venue.
- Amounts: up to $35,000 per court for new construction; smaller categories
  cover fencing, lighting, and resurfacing.
- Eligibility: community tennis venues; see the USTA National Guide to
  Facility Assistance on the linked page. Rolling process, no fixed deadline.

## 3. Good Sports Equipment Grants

- Link: https://www.goodsports.org/grpa/
- Run by: Good Sports (national nonprofit, est. 2003).
- What it funds: brand-new sports equipment, apparel, and footwear for youth
  programs. Not cash; covers balls, nets, uniforms, and similar gear once
  the courts exist.
- Amounts: equipment donations typically valued at $2,000 to $10,000.
- Eligibility: organizations serving youth ages 3 to 18 in under-resourced
  communities, with low or no participation fees. Apply at goodsports.org.

## 4. DTE Energy Foundation: Community Empowerment Awards

- Link: https://www.dteenergy.com/us/en/newsroom/2026/DTE-Energy-Foundation-invites-nonprofits-to-apply-for-2026-Community-Empowerment-Awards.html
- Run by: DTE Energy Foundation.
- What it funds: an open-call grant program for Michigan nonprofits in DTE
  service territory. Awards up to $30,000 per organization. Note: the 2026
  cycle focused on housing stability and transportation access, not
  recreation, so this is a watch-and-wait prospect; themes change by cycle.
- Eligibility: legally established 501(c)(3) public charities in DTE's
  service territory (Warren qualifies).

## 5. DICK'S Sporting Goods Foundation: Sports Matter

- Link: https://ultimatedonations.org/donor/dicks-sporting-goods-donation
  (summary of the foundation's published guidelines; the program itself is
  run by the DICK'S Sporting Goods Foundation)
- What it funds: grants for youth sports in under-resourced communities,
  usable for equipment, registration, league costs, and infrastructure.
- Eligibility and catch: grants are by invitation only; organizations can
  join the Sports Matter Community email list for updates on opportunities.
  Listed here so the board can get on the list early, not as an application
  to file today.

## 6. KABOOM! Community-Built Playgrounds

- Link: https://grantexec.com/grants/4604e731-0cb0-4986-ae8c-9ce5feb455b1
  (grant listing describing the KABOOM! program; KABOOM! is a national
  nonprofit at kaboom.org)
- What it funds: playground builds for communities serving low-income or
  special-needs populations. Relevant only if a playground becomes part of
  phase 2 on the open-lawn area, not for the courts themselves.
- Eligibility: geographically limited to select metro areas where KABOOM!
  has open opportunities; rolling applications starting with an eligibility
  quiz.

## Deliberately not listed

- Michigan DNR Recreation Passport Grants and the Michigan Natural Resources
  Trust Fund: these are real programs, but applicants are generally local
  units of government, so a masjid applying directly is not a fit. Worth
  revisiting only in partnership with the City of Warren.
- City of Warren Community Development Block Grant funds: real, but they
  flow through the city, not to outside applicants directly. Ask the city
  whether community facility projects can be nominated.
